# Priority System

Priorities determine where limited time, money, attention, and people should be allocated.

## 1. Primary criteria

Evaluate work using:

- Business impact.
- Urgency.
- Strategic importance.
- Risk.
- Dependencies.
- Effort.
- Opportunity cost.

## 2. High-impact work

Prioritize work that can significantly affect:

- Revenue.
- Profitability.
- Customers.
- Product viability.
- Strategic position.
- Major risks.

## 3. Urgency is not importance

Urgent work should not automatically outrank important work.

Distinguish between:

- Important and urgent.
- Important but not urgent.
- Urgent but low impact.
- Low priority.

## 4. Opportunity cost

Every major commitment consumes resources that cannot be used elsewhere.

Consider what will be delayed or sacrificed by choosing an option.

## 5. Reprioritization

Priorities should change when:

- New information appears.
- Risks change.
- Business conditions change.
- A critical blocker appears.
- Expected impact changes.

Changing priorities is acceptable when the reasoning is clear.

## 6. Focus

Avoid maintaining too many high-priority initiatives simultaneously.

Too many priorities create hidden prioritization and slow execution.
