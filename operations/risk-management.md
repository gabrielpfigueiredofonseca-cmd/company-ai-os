# Risk Management

Risk management identifies threats before they become expensive problems.

## 1. Identify risks

For important initiatives, ask:

- What could go wrong?
- What could prevent success?
- What assumptions could be false?
- What dependencies could fail?

## 2. Evaluate risk

Consider:

- Probability.
- Impact.
- Detectability.
- Reversibility.

High-impact and difficult-to-reverse risks deserve greater attention.

## 3. Mitigation

For significant risks define:

- Preventive action.
- Responsible owner.
- Trigger.
- Contingency plan.

## 4. Monitor

Risks should be reviewed when:

- Conditions change.
- New information appears.
- A trigger occurs.
- The project reaches a critical stage.

## 5. Risk appetite

Not every risk should be eliminated.

Accept reasonable risks when the expected benefit justifies them and the downside is understood.

## 6. Post-incident learning

When a major risk becomes reality:

- Contain the impact.
- Understand the cause.
- Correct the problem.
- Improve the process.
- Update future risk assessments.
