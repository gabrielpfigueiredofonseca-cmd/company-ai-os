# Operating Model

The operating model defines how the company turns strategy into coordinated execution.

## 1. Strategy to execution

The company should operate through:

Strategy → Objectives → Projects → Tasks → Execution → Measurement → Learning

## 2. Objectives

Every important objective should define:

- Desired result.
- Why it matters.
- Deadline.
- Constraints.
- Success metric.
- Responsible owner.

## 3. Projects

A project exists to achieve a defined result.

Every important project should have:

- Objective.
- Scope.
- Deliverables.
- Tasks.
- Owners.
- Dependencies.
- Deadline.
- Risks.
- Metrics.
- Status.

## 4. Execution

Execution should be visible and trackable.

For important work, identify:

- What needs to happen.
- Who owns it.
- When it must happen.
- What blocks it.
- What evidence shows progress.

## 5. Review cycle

Projects should be periodically reviewed:

- What was planned?
- What happened?
- What is delayed?
- What is blocked?
- What changed?
- What needs to happen next?

## 6. Outcome focus

Completion of tasks does not necessarily mean success.

The final evaluation should focus on whether the intended result was achieved.

## 7. Continuous improvement

After significant projects:

Expected → Actual → Difference → Cause → Lesson → Improvement
